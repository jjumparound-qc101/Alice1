## The chapter in places
1. **The mushroom** in the thick wood, at three-inch Alice's height (Chapter IV's wood set): the conversation with the Caterpillar, and "You are old, Father William".
2. **Above the treetops**: Alice's neck rises "like a stalk out of a sea of green leaves"; the Pigeon on her nest in "the highest tree in the wood".
3. **An open place in the wood** with "a little house in it about four feet high" (the Duchess's house; Chapter VI begins there).

## Father William: Tenniel's plates as they are (owner, 2026-10-10)
The poem is a story inside the story, and Tenniel drew exactly four pictures for its four questions. Rather than drawing new art, **show Tenniel's four plates themselves**, one per verse pair, as framed engravings that turn like book pages while the poem is recited. They contain no Alice, so there's no clash with our Alice. They need high-resolution scans (the Victorian Web images are 432 px tall). Until then, the web images can stand in at a small size. If you prefer new drawings, prompts are included (marked "if not using the plates").

## Colour
- **The Caterpillar**: blue throughout (COLOR.md rule 1).
- **"a sea of green leaves"**, "all that green stuff": the treetops seen from Alice's long neck are green, those treetops only (rule 2).
- **The poem's "your hair has become very white", "grey locks"**: coloured words in the text strip only (rule 6). Father William's hair stays ink, as in Tenniel.
