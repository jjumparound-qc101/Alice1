## The chapter in places
The hall is gone: "the great hall, with the glass table and the little door, had vanished completely." Chapter IV moves through five places, so it needs more set art than Chapters II and III:
1. **Outside** (the Rabbit hunting for his gloves; Alice sent off as "Mary Ann"): a lane or garden path. The same garden as 3 below, seen from the path.
2. **The Rabbit's house, inside**: the tidy little room upstairs, with the table in the window, the looking-glass, the fireplace, the door. Alice grows until she fills it (plate 4.1).
3. **The Rabbit's house, outside**: the neat little house with the brass plate "W. RABBIT", its garden with cucumber-frames, the chimney, the roof, the ladders. Alice's giant arm comes out of the window (plate 4.2); Bill shoots out of the chimney (plate 4.3). The crowd of little animals waits here.
4. **The thick wood**: tall grass, thistles and flowers at a tiny Alice's height, where the enormous puppy plays (plate 4.4). A buttercup she leans against.
5. **The mushroom**, with the Caterpillar on top (the chapter's last lines; Chapter V carries on there).

## Colour (owner, 2026-10-10: the plate is brass-coloured)
- **"a large blue caterpillar"** (the chapter's last sentence): the Caterpillar is blue from here on (COLOR.md rule 1). Only his first moment is in this chapter; most of his art belongs to Chapter V's list.
- **"a bright brass plate with the name 'W. RABBIT'"**: COLOR.md lists brass as an open question. **Recommendation: ink.** Brass is a material, not a stated colour, and rule 7 already says "bright" is not a colour. The alternative is a soft brass gold on the plate only.

## Sounds (no art)
Much of the middle of the chapter is heard, not seen, from inside the room: the shriek and broken glass, the voices with the ladders, "Heads below!" and the crash, "There goes Bill!" The narration carries them; the staging cuts outside for the moments Tenniel drew (the Rabbit in the frame, Bill in the air).
